# Torn Veil Online

## Canonical Architecture & Design Constitution

**Status:** Canonical
**Purpose:** Foundational design authority for Torn Veil Online
**Audience:** Human developers, Codex, Claude, Fable, future AI agents, tools, contributors, and any system participating in development
**Scope:** The conceptual universe, simulation architecture, artificial-world philosophy, and invariants that future implementation must preserve

---

## 1. The Vision

Torn Veil Online is a persistent artificial universe that can be inhabited through a role-playing game.

It is not primarily a story generator.

It is not primarily a voxel game.

It is not a conventional MMORPG with AI-powered NPC dialogue.

It is not a collection of scripted quests surrounded by procedural filler.

It is an attempt to construct a persistent artificial reality in which:

* people exist independently of the player;
* history accumulates through actual events;
* individuals perceive only limited information;
* beliefs can differ from truth;
* memories influence future decisions;
* relationships evolve through experience;
* resources create incentives;
* factions pursue interests;
* cultures develop interpretations of reality;
* classes and power structures arise from underlying mechanics;
* institutions form because they are useful;
* conflicts have causes;
* civilizations grow, fracture, migrate, conquer, worship, trade, and collapse;
* gods and supernatural entities participate in the same causal reality;
* worlds can possess different metaphysical rules;
* multiple worlds can coexist within a larger universe;
* events may eventually cross boundaries between worlds;
* computational attention follows historical importance;
* and stories emerge because all of these systems interact.

The ultimate aspiration is:

> The world should create histories that even its developers could not have predicted.

The player does not cause reality to exist.

The player enters reality.

---

## 2. The Fundamental Test

A core benchmark for Torn Veil is:

> If the player never appeared, would something interesting still happen?

Eventually, it must be possible to initialize a world, simulate it without a player for years or centuries, and discover:

* friendships;
* marriages;
* rivalries;
* murders;
* inventions;
* religions;
* migrations;
* political movements;
* economic crises;
* guilds;
* wars;
* heroes;
* tyrants;
* monsters;
* dynasties;
* fallen civilizations;
* legendary artifacts;
* ascensions;
* schisms;
* myths;
* and other meaningful historical structures

that were not individually authored in advance.

A stronger future benchmark is:

> Run two universes from different seeds for one thousand simulated years. Their histories should be structurally plausible but substantially different.

---

## 3. Simulation Before Story

Torn Veil does not fundamentally generate stories.

It generates causal reality.

Stories are interpretations of that reality.

The canonical order is:

```
WORLD RULES
    ↓
ENTITIES
    ↓
MOTIVATIONS + CONDITIONS
    ↓
DECISIONS
    ↓
ACTIONS
    ↓
EVENTS
    ↓
CONSEQUENCES
    ↓
HISTORY
    ↓
INTERPRETATION
    ↓
STORIES
```

There should not be a central system whose primary job is:

```
GenerateInterestingQuest()
```

Instead:

```
farm output falls
    ↓
food prices increase
    ↓
poor households struggle
    ↓
theft increases
    ↓
guards become overwhelmed
    ↓
merchant faction demands intervention
    ↓
political conflict increases
    ↓
a radical leader gains support
```

A historian, bard, NPC, player, or LLM may later call this:

> "The Ashford Hunger Crisis."

The name is interpretation.

The events are history.

---

## 4. Canonical Reality

The simulation owns truth.

If canonical state says:

> Vex killed Tomas at 18:43 near the eastern bridge.

then that happened.

But this does not imply everyone knows it happened.

Canonical truth must be separated from:

* perception;
* knowledge;
* belief;
* rumor;
* memory;
* interpretation;
* ideology;
* propaganda;
* mythology.

This distinction is constitutional.

---

## 5. Truth, Perception, Knowledge, Belief

Every intelligent entity experiences reality locally.

The chain is:

```
CANONICAL TRUTH
      ↓
PERCEPTION
      ↓
KNOWLEDGE / EVIDENCE
      ↓
BELIEF
      ↓
MEMORY
      ↓
INTERPRETATION
      ↓
DECISION
```

Example:

Canonical truth:

> Vex killed Tomas.

Possible minds:

**Mara**

> "I watched Vex kill Tomas."

**Rowan**

> "Mara told me Vex killed Tomas."

**Petra**

> "I heard someone was killed near the bridge."

**Vex**

> "Tomas attacked me first. I defended myself."

**A priest**

> "Tomas's death fulfilled the warning given at the shrine."

**Someone in another town**

> knows nothing.

These are not different realities.

They are different models of the same reality.

No implementation may bypass this distinction merely because the simulation internally knows the answer.

---

## 6. Knowledge Must Have Provenance

Knowledge must normally carry evidence describing how it was obtained.

Possible provenance includes:

* witnessed;
* heard;
* told;
* read;
* recorded;
* inferred;
* remembered;
* divined;
* sensed;
* prior knowledge;
* culturally inherited;
* magically transmitted;
* system-revealed.

Knowledge may have:

* confidence;
* source;
* source reliability;
* transmission hops;
* timestamp;
* associated entities;
* contradictions;
* competing interpretations.

Future systems may support field-level evidence histories.

An NPC who heard an attack but did not identify the attacker must not magically develop hostility toward the objectively correct attacker.

---

## 7. Memory Is Not a Transcript

People should not remember everything equally.

Memory must eventually account for some combination of:

* significance;
* recency;
* emotional intensity;
* repetition;
* personal relevance;
* trauma;
* social importance;
* rehearsal;
* cultural reinforcement;
* forgetting;
* distortion.

Memory influences future decisions.

A person who was humiliated ten years ago may still behave differently because of it.

A ruler may forget a minor promise.

A culture may remember an event incorrectly for centuries.

Canonical history remains distinct from remembered history.

---

## 8. Identity Is Not a Body

This is a foundational ontology rule.

Never assume:

```
one entity = one body = one coordinate
```

Instead:

```
ENTITY
   ↓
zero or more
MANIFESTATIONS
```

An ordinary mortal may have:

```
1 identity
1 body
1 current physical location
```

A deceased person may have:

```
1 identity
0 living bodies
historical relationships
memories held by others
property provenance
descendants
```

A god may have:

```
1 identity
multiple avatars
dream manifestations
temple manifestations
symbolic manifestations
remote projections
```

An Astral Being may not possess a conventional physical body at all.

This separation must be preserved throughout the architecture.

---

## 9. The Player Is an Entity, Not an Exception

Whenever reasonable, anything fundamentally possible for the player should also be possible for appropriate simulated entities.

If the player can:

* learn magic;
* gain a class;
* found a guild;
* become wealthy;
* marry;
* establish a religion;
* become a ruler;
* acquire an artifact;
* discover another world;
* ascend;
* become a god;

then a sufficiently appropriate non-player entity should eventually be able to do the same.

Different interfaces and computational strategies may be used.

The ontology should remain shared.

The player should not possess metaphysical privileges simply because they are controlled by a human unless such privileges are explicitly part of that world's reality.

## 10. Motivations Before Scripts

Characters should act primarily because of motivations, beliefs, circumstances, relationships, capabilities, and goals.

General motivations may include:

* survival;
* safety;
* hunger;
* comfort;
* wealth;
* status;
* belonging;
* family;
* friendship;
* love;
* sex;
* revenge;
* justice;
* duty;
* faith;
* knowledge;
* curiosity;
* power;
* autonomy;
* legacy;
* pleasure;
* fear;
* ideology.

Behavior should emerge from combinations of these.

A bandit should not attack because:

```
bandit.hostile = true
```

A bandit may rob because:

```
needs resources
+
target appears vulnerable
+
guards absent
+
personal risk acceptable
+
group norms permit robbery
```

Violence is a means, not a universal goal.

## 11. Conflict Must Have Intent

Hostility is not identical to lethal intent.

Conflict should support intentions such as:

* avoid;
* threaten;
* intimidate;
* rob;
* extort;
* defend;
* capture;
* arrest;
* subdue;
* drive away;

Escalation should emerge from circumstances.

```
disagreement
→ confrontation
→ threat
→ resistance
→ fight
→ surrender / retreat / incapacitation
→ possible lethal escalation
```

Death should mean something because it is not the automatic result of every confrontation.

```
capabilities

## 13. Power Ontology

Torn Veil uses a conceptual hierarchy inspired by progression fantasy.

The current working hierarchy is:

```
Normal
Iron
Bronze
Silver
Gold
Diamond
God
Astral King
Astral Being
──────────────────────
John Smith
```

Names below John Smith may later be changed to original Torn Veil terminology.

The structural concept is canonical.

---

## 14. Power Tiers Are Summaries, Not Rules

A tier summarizes an entity's relationship with reality.

The simulation must never decide:

> "Gold beats Silver because Gold is higher."

Instead it evaluates actual:

* capabilities;
* abilities;
* resources;
* vulnerabilities;
* conditions;
* environment;
* preparation;
* tactics;
* metaphysical interactions.

Therefore unusual situations remain possible.

Examples:

* a Bronze specialist may counter a Silver opponent;
* a Normal person may possess an artifact capable of harming a Gold;
* a Diamond entity may be vulnerable to a specific metaphysical condition;
* a god's avatar may intentionally operate at Iron-level physical capability.

Tier is classification.

Capability is truth.

---

## 15. Meaning of the Power Tiers

**Normal**

Ordinary mortal existence.

Normal entities can still become historically significant through:

* politics;
* wealth;
* invention;
* religion;
* leadership;
* culture;
* strategy.

Power rank is not historical importance.

**Iron**

Clearly beyond ordinary mortal capability.

Early supernatural development.

**Bronze**

Established supernatural individual capable of surviving environments and threats ordinary people cannot.

**Silver**

Regional strategic power.

A Silver individual may influence:

* cities;
* military plans;
* political alliances;
* major organizations.

**Gold**

National or geopolitical power.

The presence of a Gold entity may alter military doctrine and diplomacy.

**Diamond**

Civilization-shaping or near-mythic existence.

Diamond entities may:

* survive for centuries;
* destroy armies;
* create lasting artifacts;
* reshape regions;
* become central to religions and histories.

**God**

Godhood is not merely another numeric advancement tier.

It represents an ontological transition.

Godhood may involve:

* domains;
* worship;
* conceptual authority;
* metaphysical contracts;
* divine inheritance;
* ascension;
* world recognition;
* manifestations.

A god may exist through multiple bodies or manifestations simultaneously.

**Astral King**

A multiversal strategic entity capable of meaningfully influencing multiple worlds or the structures between them.

Potential abilities may include:

* interworld travel;
* world claims;
* world-scale manifestations;
* subordinate divinities;
* manipulation of world boundaries;
* creation or management of cross-world structures.

**Astral Being**

A broad category for entities primarily operating above individual worlds.

Some may remain recognizable as persons.

Others may be:

* distributed consciousnesses;
* living metaphysical laws;
* entities inhabiting interworld space;
* emergent intelligences spanning civilizations;
* beings whose manifestations are worlds or stars.

## 16. John Smith

John Smith is not simply the most powerful creature.

John Smith is outside ordinary progression ontology.

The hierarchy should be conceptualized as:

```
Normal
Iron
Bronze
Silver
Gold
Diamond
God
Astral King
Astral Being
========================
SIMULATION / ONTOLOGY BOUNDARY
========================
John Smith
```

No normal progression path should simply end with:

```
Astral Being → gain XP → John Smith
```

John Smith sits above the game universe as an orchestrator.

He is intentionally a point that ordinary entities cannot realistically attain.

John Smith may eventually be detectable from inside the simulation.

Extremely advanced entities may theorize about him.

They may discover evidence that a higher-order structure exists.

But John Smith is not merely another boss.

---

## 17. John Smith's Role

John Smith represents the highest-order orchestration layer.

Conceptually his responsibilities include:

* world registry;
* world lifecycle;
* simulation scheduling;
* cognitive resource allocation;
* integrity enforcement;
* cross-world permissions;
* multiversal causal archive;
* detection of historically significant entities;
* simulation recovery;
* management of inactive or abstracted worlds.

John Smith should resemble:

```
scheduler + kernel + archivist + constitutional authority
```

more than:

```
dungeon master + storyteller.
```

He should not manufacture drama merely because the simulation appears boring.

---

## 18. John Smith Does Not Write the Story

John Smith must not arbitrarily decide:

> "This town needs excitement, therefore spawn a dragon."

If a dragon appears, there should be a legitimate causal path.

John Smith may manage computation and enforce reality.

He does not substitute authorial whims for emergence.

---

## 19. Historical Significance

Every entity may accumulate historical significance independently of raw power.

Possible dimensions include:

* number of lives affected;
* political influence;
* causal centrality;
* cultural recognition;
* economic influence;
* religious importance;
* military impact;
* interworld impact;
* relationship to major historical events.

A Normal-tier philosopher could have enormous historical significance.

A Gold-tier monster sleeping under a mountain might temporarily have very little active historical significance.

---

## 20. Three Independent Axes

Torn Veil should distinguish at least:

**Power Tier**

What can this entity accomplish mechanically?

**Cognitive Fidelity**

How deeply is the entity currently being simulated?

**Historical Significance**

How consequential has this entity become?

These dimensions must not be conflated.

Example:

```
Mara Bramble
Power: Normal
Cognition: Full
Historical Significance: 12

Emperor Tal
Power: Iron
Cognition: Deep
Historical Significance: 94

Sleeper Beneath Orun
Power: Diamond
Cognition: Dormant
Historical Significance: 68

John Smith
Power: Not Applicable
Cognition: Orchestrator
Historical Significance: Not Applicable
```

## 21. Cognitive Level of Detail

The world must eventually contain far more entities than can receive deep cognition simultaneously.

Therefore cognition uses Cognitive Level of Detail, or CLOD.

An entity may dynamically move between cognitive levels.

---

## 22. CLOD 0 — Aggregate Simulation

Used for large populations where individual minds are currently unnecessary.

Possible representation:

* demographics;
* production;
* mortality;
* migration;
* culture;
* military manpower;
* disease;
* resource consumption.

A city of 800,000 people does not require 800,000 continuously running detailed agents.

---

## 23. CLOD 1 — Lightweight Individual

A named or potentially relevant entity may track:

* identity;
* household;
* occupation;
* basic traits;
* major relationships;
* important beliefs;
* current role;
* simple motivations;
* summarized history.

Cheap enough for large populations.

---

## 24. CLOD 2 — Full Agent

Local or important characters receive:

* detailed perception;
* knowledge provenance;
* episodic memory;
* relationships;
* emotions;
* utility goals;
* plans;
* physical navigation;
* detailed actions.

Ashford Vale currently approximates this layer.

---

## 25. CLOD 3 — Deep Cognition

Historically important or closely observed entities may receive expensive cognition.

This may involve an LLM.

Possible functions:

* complex long-horizon planning;
* negotiation;
* interpretation;
* political strategy;
* creative problem solving;
* introspection;
* unusual tactics;
* expressive dialogue.

Canonical actions still require simulation validation.

---

## 26. CLOD 4 — Extraordinary Cognition

Reserved for entities whose ontology requires cognition beyond ordinary agents.

Examples:

* gods;
* Astral Kings;
* distributed entities;
* entities with extreme subjective time;
* entities controlling institutions across worlds.

This does not imply constant maximum computational expenditure.

---

## 27. Entities Earn Compute Through History

A core Torn Veil idea is:

> Characters become computationally important because the world makes them important.

Example:

```
farmer
↓

↓
discovers unusual power
↓
becomes militia commander
↓
wins major battle
↓
becomes general
↓
overthrows ruler
↓

The entity may progress from:

```

```

No developer had to designate them as the protagonist.

## 28. Subjective Time

Simulation time must not assume every entity experiences cognition at the same rate.

An entity may possess:

```
subjectiveTimeRate
```

A powerful being might experience:

```
1 external second
=
100 subjective seconds
```

Another entity may sleep for centuries while processing almost nothing.

Subjective time is not merely a visual slow-motion effect.

It belongs to the simulation layer.

## 30. Mechanical Truth vs Cultural Interpretation

A world may mechanically contain:

> Fire mana becomes stronger near volcanic leylines.

Different cultures may believe:

**Mage**

> "Leyline density increases fire efficiency."

**Priest**

> "The Flame God blesses these mountains."

**Villager**

> "Fire behaves strangely near the red peaks."

**Scholar**

> "Measured fire output increases approximately thirty percent."

One mechanical truth.

Several interpretations.

Cultures should be able to misunderstand reality.

## 44. LLM Constitutional Role

LLMs are allowed to make inhabitants more intelligent and expressive.

LLMs are not canonical reality.

An LLM may:

* interpret evidence;
* form hypotheses;
* propose plans;
* select among available intentions;
* negotiate;
* produce dialogue;
* introspect;
* explain reasoning;
* generate tactics;
* express emotion;
* create metaphors;
* formulate political strategies.

An LLM may not directly:

* create items;
* create money;
* teleport entities;
* change ownership;
* declare someone dead;
* award itself abilities;
* invent mechanical powers;
* alter geography;
* rewrite history;
* change relationships;
* know facts the entity has never acquired;
* ignore physical constraints;
* invent a faction membership;
* change world rules.

---

## 45. The LLM Action Contract

Example:

LLM proposes:

> "I offer Rowan twenty silver to ignore the theft."

The LLM does not perform the bribe.

It submits an intention.

Simulation validates:

```
Does speaker possess 20 silver?
Is Rowan present?
Can Rowan perceive the offer?
Can currency transfer?
Is bribery possible under current rules?
How does Rowan evaluate the proposal?
```

Then canonical events occur.

The LLM may interpret those results afterward.

The architecture is:

```
LLM
↓
PROPOSED INTENT
↓
SIMULATION VALIDATION
↓
CANONICAL ACTION
↓
WORLD EVENTS
↓
OBSERVATION
↓
LLM
```

Never:

```
LLM
↓
WORLD TRUTH
```

## 46. Rendering Is Not Reality

The voxel client is one presentation layer.

The simulation must not depend on Three.js, a camera, frame rate, DOM state, or a specific renderer.

Conceptually:

```
CANONICAL UNIVERSE
       │
       ├── Voxel Client
       ├── Simulation Inspector
       ├── Headless Runner
       ├── Historical Viewer
       ├── Strategic Map
       ├── Future High-Fidelity Client
       └── Future VR Interface
```

A rendering bug cannot be allowed to redefine canonical truth.

---

## 47. Simulation Clocks

The architecture should support multiple clocks.

At minimum:

* real/render time;
* physical simulation time;
* calendar/world time;
* strategic time;
* cognitive time;
* subjective entity time.

Different systems may operate at different frequencies.

There is no constitutional requirement that every entity be simulated every frame.

## 48. Scale Through Abstraction

Torn Veil should eventually scale from:

```
person
→ household
→ settlement
→ region
→ kingdom
→ civilization
→ world
→ universe
```

Higher-order simulation should not require continuously iterating every low-level entity.

Faraway systems may operate through abstractions.

When detail becomes relevant, the simulation may materialize or increase fidelity while preserving continuity.

## 49. Historical Continuity

Abstraction must not mean arbitrary reinvention.

If a distant kingdom is simulated statistically and later becomes relevant, its detailed state should be compatible with:

* prior population;
* resources;
* wars;
* dynasties;
* religions;
* migrations;
* major people;
* major events.

The engine must avoid producing mutually contradictory histories merely because fidelity changed.

---

## 50. Stable Identity

Persistent simulation requires identities that remain stable across:

* saves;
* version changes;
* abstraction levels;
* world simulation;
* historical records;
* relationships;
* item provenance.

Authored semantic entities should eventually use stable semantic identifiers rather than fragile generation-order IDs.

Generated entities require durable persistent identity.

---

## 51. Causal History

Significant events should be able to reference causes and consequences.

Example:

```
harvest failure
↓
bread prices rise
↓
theft rises
↓
guard crackdown
↓
civil unrest
↓
merchant killed
↓
guild retaliates
↓
council collapses
```

This graph is more valuable than a flat event log.

The system should preserve important causal ancestry even when compacting detailed simulation events.

---

## 52. World Chronicle

Torn Veil should eventually possess a historical interpretation layer capable of answering:

* What happened?
* Why did it happen?
* Who caused it?
* Who benefited?
* Who believes what happened?
* What were the major turning points?
* Which entities became historically significant?

An LLM may be extremely useful here.

It acts as:

```
historian
```

not:

```
retroactive author.
```

---

## 53. Developer Observability

The simulation must be observable enough to diagnose emergent behavior.

Development builds should eventually produce automatic structured telemetry including:

* entity decisions;
* goal changes;
* significant perceptions;
* knowledge transfer;
* relationship changes;
* conflicts;
* deaths;
* migrations;
* faction decisions;
* economic changes;
* causal events;
* anomalies.

The player/developer should not have to manually record every unusual event.

Observability must not alter canonical decisions.

---

## 54. Headless Simulation Is First-Class

The world must eventually be able to run without rendering.

A headless runner should support:

```
create universe
create world
advance 1 day
advance 1 year
advance 100 years
inspect results
```

This enables:

* determinism tests;
* emergence tests;
* economy tests;
* civilization tests;
* balancing;
* historical simulation;
* AI evaluation.

The renderer is not required for reality to progress.

---

## 55. Artificial-World Benchmark

A future standard benchmark should include:

Player absent.

Inspect:

* population;

* deaths;

* marriages;
* rivalries;
* factions;

* resource shortages;
* migrations;

Then repeat with another seed.

The goal is not identical outcomes.

The goal is plausible divergence.

---

## 56. No Premature Scale

The existence of a multiversal goal does not justify immediately building thousands of worlds.

The correct progression is:

```
one believable village
↓
one believable region
↓
multiple settlements
↓
world-scale systems
↓
multiple worlds
↓
multiversal systems
```

Scale without depth is failure.

Ashford Vale remains valuable as a laboratory where every entity can be inspected.

---

## 57. Depth Before Breadth

When choosing between:

```
1,000 shallow NPCs
```

and:

```
30 entities whose behavior demonstrates the intended architecture
```

prefer depth until the systems are proven.

When scaling, preserve the same conceptual rules through abstraction rather than replacing them with unrelated mechanics.

## 58. Emergent Importance

Do not assume important characters are preselected.

The simulation should permit an unknown person to become:

* guildmaster;
* general;
* emperor;
* saint;
* villain;
* legendary crafter;
* revolutionary;
* prophet;
* Diamond entity;
* god;
* Astral actor.

Historical significance should emerge.

## 59. Emergent Heroes and Villains

"Hero" and "villain" should normally be cultural interpretations.

An entity may:

* believe they are righteous;
* be worshipped by one culture;
* be hated by another;
* be remembered differently centuries later.

The engine may classify actions mechanically.

It should not require universal moral narration.

## 60. Emergent Progression Meta

Knowledge of the world's mechanics can itself become power.

Characters may discover:

* effective ability combinations;
* advancement requirements;
* monster weaknesses;
* crafting interactions;
* metaphysical loopholes;
* training methods.

Successful techniques may spread.

```
individual discovers technique
↓
teaches students
↓
school forms
↓
rivals develop counters
↓
combat culture changes
```

Game mechanics can therefore create culture.

## 61. System Discovery

Not every inhabitant must understand the mechanics governing reality.

Some may scientifically study them.

Some may interpret them religiously.

Some may receive explicit system interfaces.

MECHANICAL TRUTH
≠
SYSTEM PRESENTATION
≠
CULTURAL INTERPRETATION

Eventually causal chains may cross worlds.

Example:

↓

## 64. Unpredictability Without Random Nonsense

The goal is not maximum randomness.

The goal is:

> unpredictable consequences from understandable causes.

Good emergence:

> A food shortage caused political radicalization.

Bad emergence:

> A random table said the king turned into a chicken.

Randomness may introduce variation.

Causality gives variation meaning.

---

## 65. Composability

Major systems should interact through explicit semantic state and events.

Examples:

```
ecology affects economy
economy affects politics
politics affects warfare
warfare affects demographics
demographics affect religion
religion affects factions
factions affect individual opportunities
individuals affect history
```

Systems should not live as disconnected minigames.

---

## 66. Avoid Bespoke Character Scripting

Do not solve a desired behavior by writing:

```
if npc.name === "Mara":
    run_to_guard()
```

unless the behavior represents genuinely unique authored identity.

Prefer:

```
perceived severe crime
+
fear
+
low combat confidence
+
trust in guard institution
+
reporting opportunity
=
report goal
```

Generic systems should produce specific behavior.

* misunderstood;

## 76. Constitutional Invariants

Future agents MUST preserve the following unless a deliberate constitutional revision is approved.

**I. Simulation authority**

Canonical reality belongs to the simulation.

**II. Player non-centrality**

The world exists and evolves independently of the player.

**III. Local knowledge**

Entities do not automatically know canonical truth.

**IV. Provenance**

Important acquired knowledge should retain evidence of how it was learned.

**V. Identity separation**

Entity identity is not equivalent to one body or one coordinate.

**VI. Shared ontology**

Player and NPC entities should use the same fundamental reality where practical.

**VII. Causality**

Important consequences should arise through explicit state and events.

**VIII. Emergence over scripting**

General mechanics are preferred over bespoke scripted outcomes.

**IX. Capability over labels**

Classes and ranks summarize mechanics; they do not replace them.

**X. LLM containment**

LLMs may propose and express. They do not directly establish mechanical truth.

**XI. Cognitive scalability**

Deep cognition is dynamically allocated rather than universally required.

**XII. Historical continuity**

Abstraction must preserve compatible history.

**XIII. World independence**

Presentation layers do not own canonical simulation state.

**XIV. No player-centered universe**

Reality does not pause, scale, or become fair merely because of player presence.

**XV. John Smith boundary**

John Smith exists above ordinary progression and orchestration, not as a conventional attainable character tier.

## 79. What Success Looks Like

Torn Veil succeeds when moments like these can occur without having been scripted:

A nameless child survives the destruction of her village.

She becomes obsessed with preventing it from happening again.

She trains.

She discovers an unusual interaction between two abilities.

Others imitate her.

A combat school forms.

That school becomes a military institution.

The institution changes a war.

The war destroys an empire.

Refugees carry the school's philosophy to another continent.

Centuries later, the philosophy becomes part of a religion.

A descendant becomes a Diamond-ranked ruler.

The ruler discovers an interworld breach.

The breach exposes another world to the religion.

A god emerges from the resulting worship.

The god eventually becomes involved in an Astral conflict.

No developer wrote:

> "Quest 9284: Found the Church of the Broken Spear."

The systems created the history.

## 80. Final Principle

When making a difficult architectural choice, ask:

> Does this make Torn Veil more capable of producing a coherent world whose future we cannot completely predict?

If yes, it is probably aligned with the project.

If a feature makes the game more conventional but weakens that possibility, reconsider it.

The goal is not merely to make an RPG with good AI.

The goal is to build:

> a persistent artificial universe capable of creating its own history, inhabited by entities who know only pieces of the truth, shaped by systems that interact across individuals, societies, worlds, gods, and eventually realities themselves—while John Smith watches from above the boundary of the game.
