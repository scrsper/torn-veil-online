# Ashford cultural garment set v0.1

Machine: Bernhaldt. Branch `claude/ashford-cultural-garments-v0-1`, cut from PR #48's remote head
`709e6b5`. Everything below was measured on this machine on 21 September 2026.

PR #48 finished with a precise diagnosis: the Character Foundry works, and every Ashford resident
is wearing modern business clothing, because no installed pack contains this culture's wardrobe.
Its own recommendation was *"get one culturally correct garment set onto the City Sample body rig,
and nothing else."* That is what this is.

---

## 1. What was built

Eleven garments, each cut for all six City Sample fit families — 66 meshes. Original geometry,
generated by `art/tools/ashford_garments/`, skinned to `SK_Base`.

| Piece | Slot | Tags the Foundry matches on | Triangles |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Kosode_Work** | upperGarment | kimono, tunic, work, peasant | 2,140 |
| **Kosode_Wide** | upperGarment | kimono, tunic, robe, wrap, formal | 2,252 |
| **Haori** | upperGarment | coat, kimono | 1,392 |
| **Hakama** | lowerGarment | hakama, trousers | 876 |
| **Hakama_Short** | lowerGarment | hakama, trousers, short | 876 |
| **MoSkirt** | lowerGarment | skirt, robe | 476 |
| **Obi** | accessory | obi, belt, wrap | 408 |
| **Maekake** (work apron) | accessory | apron | 476 |
| **Geta** | footwear | sandals, shoes | 640 |
| **Waraji** | footwear | sandals, shoes | 576 |
| **TabiBoot** | footwear | boots | 576 |

The four the brief asked for are the kosode, the hakama, the obi and the geta. The other seven
exist because the resolver was already asking for them by name and would otherwise have fallen
through to City Sample content: `coat` (travel and fur silhouettes), `skirt`/`robe` (layered,
formal, ceremonial and ascetic), `boots` (travelling and armoured), `apron` (trades), and a short
hakama for working registers.

### The grammar, and where it came from

`art/tools/ashford_garments/garments.py` documents this in full. In short, what the eight
reference figures share: a crossed-front wrapped upper garment with a collar band and a pale
under-collar; a broad sash that is always the brightest thing worn; two sleeve registers that
track status rather than sex; volume below the waist, either divided and pleated or a single
wrapped length; thonged sandals over split-toe foot coverings, never a closed shoe.

Every kosode is hip length. That is a deliberate constraint: the lower garment always supplies
the silhouette below the waist, so any kosode combines with any lower garment without one passing
through the other, and status is carried by the sleeve and the cloth instead of by the hem.

### What was deliberately not built

- **The off-shoulder necklines** (hana, yuki, the dancer). An open neckline relies on the
  MetaHuman face mesh for chest skin, and the vendor's own open necklines stop at z = 1.405 —
  about the collarbone. Every kosode here closes at the throat.
- **Armour.** Kaito's lamellar and the ronin's cuirass are a slot the resolver already asks for
  and would have doubled the slice without dressing one extra resident.
- **Faces, hair, weapons, jewellery, tattoos.** Not a wardrobe.

## 2. Three things had to be measured first, and two contradicted our own notes

`art/tools/ashford_garments/measure_fit.py`, output in `.debug/ashford-garments/fit-measurements.json`.

**`*_body` is not a nude body. It is two hands.** 10,942 vertices spanning 18 cm of height; every
top-weighted bone is a finger. `REAL_ASSET_VALIDATION.md` §2 states these are complete nude
bodies — that is wrong. (`audit_character_assets.py` already had it right in a comment; the prose
doc contradicted the code.) The real modular contract, read off the vendor meshes' own material
sections:

| mesh | geometry | skin it carries |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `*_body` | two hands | hands |
| `*_FaceMesh` | head | head, neck, upper chest |
| `*_turtleneck` etc. | torso + arms | none |
| `*_slacks` / `*_skirt` | legs | **bare feet**, as a second section |
| `*_oxfords` etc. | a shoe shell over those feet | none |

So a garment replacing the trousers has to bring feet with it. Copying the vendor's feet is not an
option — that geometry is licensed. Every Ashford lower garment therefore ends at the ankle and
every piece of Ashford footwear includes a **tabi**: correct under geta, original geometry, and it
means the set is complete on its own.

**The six builds share `SK_Base` but not a bind pose.** Female and male differ by 11 cm of
stature; the male arm sits 6 cm higher and 9 cm further out. Measuring the rig once and applying it
to both put every male sleeve reading at a flat 12 cm. Garments are authored per bind pose and per
girth: six fits, exactly as the vendor does it.

**Skeletal FBX export asserts in a headless commandlet** on a null render `MeshObject`
(`SkinnedMeshComponent.cpp:4987`), so `Run-EditorPython.ps1` gained `-Render`
(`-AllowCommandletRendering`). MetaHuman face meshes cannot be FBX-exported at all on this engine
version — they index past the end of their section array. The vendor's own open necklines supplied
the one measurement we needed from them instead.

## 3. How it is skinned

Weights are transferred from the vendor garments — which are already correctly weighted to this
build's bind pose and to the 170-bone twist and correction chains City Sample's deformation relies
on — sampled at a **bind proxy** that hugs the body rather than at the garment's real position. A
sleeve hanging 30 cm off the arm has no sensible nearest-surface weight; the point on the arm it
hangs from does. That one indirection is what lets a wide furisode sleeve and a pleated hakama
deform like cloth instead of like a flag nailed to the nearest bone.

Nothing derived from vendor *geometry* is exported. Weights are numbers, not shapes.

## 4. Deformation

`art/tools/ashford_garments/deform_test.py`, renders in `.debug/ashford-garments/deform/`.

Each outfit is posed through stride, sprint, crouch, a guard stance and a thrown punch, and
compared against its own rest state. The metric is how far the body pokes outside the garment that
wraps it; the absolute number is dominated by body that is simply near a garment without being
wrapped by it, so what is reported is the **change from rest** — which is what "does not explode
when walking" means.

| | stride | sprint | crouch | guard | punch |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| female_nrw, worst piece | 0.2 mm | 0.3 mm | −0.3 mm | 0.9 mm | 0.4 mm |
| male_nrw, worst piece | 0.5 mm | 0.3 mm | −1.6 mm | 0.3 mm | 0.2 mm |

Worst change across both sexes and all five poses: **1.6 mm**. No piece detaches, inverts or
separates.

## 5. Materials

One material, `M_TV_AshfordCloth`, and four instances of it — one per region — assigned to the
material slots the generator paints onto every mesh:

| slot | takes | wear strength |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `TV_Cloth` | `Tint` — the garment's own colour | 0.16 |
| `TV_Hem` | `Tint`, dirtier: hems and knees | 0.55 |
| `TV_Accent` | `Accent` — collar band, obi, sandal thong | 0.10 |
| `TV_Under` | the pale under-layer inside the collar | 0.12 |

`Tint`, `Accent`, `Wear` and `Grooming` are pushed by the presentation layer that already existed.
`Accent` is new: the canonical palette has carried an accent colour all along and
`UTVCharacterPresentation::ApplyTints` was discarding it for every slot that was not an accessory.
That single addition is what lets one mesh be a muted indigo kosode with a vermilion collar
without authoring that combination as an asset.

Colourways are not authored. Sixteen canonical `GARMENT_PALETTES` supply primary, secondary and
accent per costume family, and `weatheredColour` has already aged them by the person's `wear`
before the tint is ever pushed. Eight of the sixteen are in use across the Ashford 33.

### Four defects the materials went through, each silent

1. **No `bUsedWithSkeletalMesh`.** The material is assigned, compiles, reports no error, and is
   substituted for Epic's grey default at draw time. One line in the PIE log says so.
2. **Vertex colours never arrived.** The regions were vertex colours first. UE 5.8 imports FBX
   through Interchange, and Interchange ignores `FbxImportUI`'s `vertex_color_import_option`
   entirely. The generator wrote all four masks, the exported FBX was verified to carry them, the
   engine received none, and nothing in the log said so. They are material slots now, because a
   missing section is visible.
3. **Wear was lightening dark cloth.** Blending toward a mid-brown lifted a near-black charcoal
   kosode (`0x22222a`) to mid grey — dirt making the darkest cloth in the palette three times
   brighter — and double-counted, since `weatheredColour` had already weathered it. Wear now
   darkens.
4. **Rebuilding the material standalone deleted it and recreated it**, which nulled the reference
   in all four instances and all 66 meshes; the settlement rendered in `WorldGridMaterial`'s grey
   checkerboard. It is rebuilt in place now.

## 6. Foundry mapping

No asset path enters canonical `Person` state, and none entered the manifest. The simulation still
says `kosode / working-class / worn / muted-blue`; the classification and the resolver do the rest.

```text
Person.appearance          ->  garmentSilhouette, status, wear, palette, roleCues
slotRules()  (manifest)    ->  "upperGarment: [kimono], prefer [work, female, peasant]"
audit_character_assets.py  ->  SKM_TV_Kosode_Work_female_nrw: [kimono, tunic, work, peasant,
                               female], fits [city:female:nrw]
resolveSlot()              ->  that mesh, on that build
M_TV_AshfordCloth          ->  Tint = palette.primary, Accent = palette.accent, Wear = person.wear
```

Manifest changes, all read off the reference sheets rather than off the catalogue:

- **Layered and formal dress asks for a wrapped lower layer, not a divided one.** The fighting and
  working figures wear pleated hakama; the layered and ceremonial ones wear a single length to the
  ankle. Asking both for `hakama` put every woman at a festival in a warrior's trousers.
- **No Ashford silhouette populates `GarmentShape.robe`.** All eight figures wear an upper layer
  over a separate lower one, and requesting a robe alongside them would resolve all three and stack
  two garments on one torso — `covers` is honoured only from the body entry.
- **The sash is requested directly** by any wrapped silhouette, rather than waiting for a
  `waist_cord` accessory. It is how the costume is fastened, not an ornament. `belt` drops out of
  the carried set when it is, or the same obi resolved twice.
- **`modern` is forbidden** on every garment slot. City Sample dresses a contemporary city; that is
  a true description of the asset, and left as a mere preference it lost a coin flip and put one
  resident in a business skirt under her own apron. Forbidden tags are dropped only after every
  relaxation step, so a machine whose only clothing is modern still dresses the whole settlement —
  and reports that it relaxed.

### A bug this found in the classifier

`('rags', r'...torn...')` matched **TornVeil**. Every asset under `/Game/TornVeil/` was tagged
`rags`, and `rags` is one of only two disqualifying tags in the manifest — so the first character
content this project ever owned would have been silently refused for every resident above `poor`.
`\b` was no help either: it does not fire between an underscore and a letter, so `\btorn\b` then
missed `SK_Torn_Cloak`. Both use letter lookarounds now, with a regression test.

#### The defect this pass found: a haori worn with nothing under it

Checking role appropriateness across all twenty occupations turned up a miller, a woodcutter and
a bandit whose *only* upper garment was a **haori** — an open-fronted over-layer. It carried
`coat` and `kimono` honestly, so it answered those requests and won the tie-break.

On this body that is not a styling error. City Sample bodies are **hands only**, so there is no
torso under an open coat: rendered alone the haori is a shell with a void where the chest should
be, and only the face mesh’s neck and upper chest keep it from being obvious head-on. It was in
every PIE frame this slice first captured, on one resident — which is why those frames were
re-shot rather than kept.

The haori is an **accessory** now, so it can only ever be worn over something, and the travelling
silhouettes take a kosode underneath: in this culture a kosode is what you travel in and the
haori goes over it. A second tie-break went the same way — a plain `kimono` request for the
`hakama_set` silhouette now prefers the *working* cut, which had been putting a miller at a
millstone in hanging sleeves. Both are pinned by tests.

## 8. Performance

Measured from the AssetRegistry after import; `.debug/ashford-garments/import-report.json`.

| outfit | triangles | material slots | skeletal components | LODs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| work: kosode + hakama + obi + waraji | **4,000** | 11 | 4 | 1 |
| festival: wide kosode + mo + obi + geta | 3,776 | 10 | 4 | 1 |
| worker: + apron, short hakama | 4,476 | 13 | 5 | 1 |
| **the City Sample outfit it replaces** | **47,006** | 4 | 3 | 4 |

An Ashford resident's clothing is **11.8× cheaper in triangles** than the modern outfit it
replaces. Two honest costs against that:

- **No LODs.** Every Ashford mesh is LOD0 only; the vendor garments ship four. At 4,000 triangles
  the Ashford outfit is still cheaper than the vendor's *LOD3*, but it does not get cheaper with
  distance, and a crowd of 127 would want that.
- **More material slots**, 11 against 4, so more draw calls per resident. That is the price of
  putting the region masks in sections instead of in vertex colours, which was not a choice.

## 9. Reference fidelity

`reference/hana-vs-result.jpg`, `kaito-vs-result.jpg`, `ascetic-vs-result.jpg`. Rendered in
Blender on the same City Sample bind pose the engine uses, in each family's canonical palette —
not PIE screenshots, because the sheets are front-on studio figures and a resident is neither on
demand. No reconstruction is claimed.

**What reads correctly.** The silhouette: crossed front, collar band, broad sash, volume below the
waist. The two sleeve registers. The palette — festival crimson with gold, ronin charcoal with
vermilion, ascetic bone with crimson — all read as their family. Footwear: thonged sandals over
tabi, and the geta's raised teeth. The ascetic is the closest of the three; a bone-white wide-
sleeved kosode with a crimson collar and obi over a dark wrapped lower layer is recognisably the
wandering monk's costume.

**What remains weak.**

- **The hem is one length.** Every reference figure wears layered, ragged, overlapping hems; every
  Ashford lower garment ends in one clean line.
- **No pattern.** All three references are covered in floral and geometric brocade. Ashford cloth
  is flat colour. This is the single largest gap between the two columns.
- **The obi is a flat band.** Hana's and yuki's are sculpted, knotted and hung with cord and
  tassels; ours is a smooth ring with a bundle at the back.
- **The MoSkirt has no drape** — it is a smooth column where the reference has a front slit and
  falling layers.
- **No trim.** The gold edging on every reference collar and hem is not there.
- **The shoulders still read slightly padded** where the sleeve root meets the body tube.

## 10. What is committed

Generated content is not. `unreal/TornVeilOnline/Content/TornVeil/Characters/Ashford/` is 19 MB of
meshes that embed the City Sample clothing skeleton's reference pose and cannot load without that
licensed pack — the same call `Content/TornVeil/LocalPalette/` already makes, recorded in
`.gitignore` rather than in a machine-local exclude. Rebuild with:

```bash
blender --background --python art/tools/ashford_garments/build_garments.py
```
```bash
unreal/scripts/Run-EditorPython.ps1 -Script unreal/scripts/import_ashford_garments.py -Render
```

`M_TV_AshfordCloth` and its four instances **are** committed: no vendor data, 12 KB, and the file
that decides what this culture looks like belongs in review. Re-running the importer rebuilds
them, so expect them to show as modified after an import.

## 12. Remaining gaps

Observed, not speculative.

1. **Eight of thirty-three residents cannot be dressed by any wardrobe.** They are on Polytope and
   Quantum bodies whose clothing is baked into the body mesh. Replacing those bodies with City
   Sample ones — or building Ashford clothing for those two rigs — is a separate slice.
2. **No LODs.** Every Ashford mesh is LOD0 only.
3. **No pattern, no trim, one hem length.** §9. The largest visual distance from the references.
4. **Faces do not attach in the standalone arena.** Pre-existing, PR #48's, unchanged.
5. **`city:female:ovw` is unexercised.** Built and imported; no resident in the Ashford sample
   resolves to it.
6. **The shoulder reads slightly padded** where the sleeve root meets the body tube.
7. **`armor` is still never met.** A lamellar-armoured resident resolves to a kosode and hakama
   with no armour over them. (`robe` is never requested, by design — §6.)
9. **The smith reads oddly** (§7, role coverage): wide sleeves and a wrapped skirt at a forge.
   That silhouette is canonical, so the fix belongs in the appearance layer rather than here.
8. **Group photography is limited by the bridge's streaming radius**, not by the wardrobe.

## 13. Verdict

**Yes — Ashford now reads as its own culture, at the level of costume.**

Ten residents in one frame, every one of them in a crossed-front kosode with a collar band, a
sash at the waist, wide divided trousers or a wrapped skirt, and thonged sandals over split-toe
tabi. Not one of them is in a business suit. Across the canonical thirty-three, every resident a
wardrobe can dress is dressed from this set — 25 of 25, with zero foreign garments, fourteen
distinct outfits and no group of more than four alike. It is not ten identical kimono NPCs and it
is not modern-business Ashford.

It is also not finished, and the distance left is easy to state: this is the *cut* of the culture's
clothing without its *surface*. The references are covered in brocade, gold trim and layered
ragged hems; Ashford cloth is flat colour with one clean edge. Standing next to hana or kaito the
silhouette, the layering, the palette and the footwear all read — and the fabric does not.

The smallest next slice is therefore not another garment. It is **texture**: a tiled cloth weave, a
small set of stencilled motifs driven by the palette that already exists, and trim on the collar
and hem. Second after that, LODs — 66 meshes at LOD0 is the one number here that will not survive
a settlement of 127.
